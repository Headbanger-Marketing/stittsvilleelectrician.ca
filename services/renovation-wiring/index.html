Renovation wiring in Stittsville.

A design change after rough-in, such as moving a kitchen island or a doorway, can leave wiring that no longer matches the plan in a Stittsville renovation. The work already done needs checking before walls close.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Stittsville property

Gather the drawings from before and after the change, and mark every wall, ceiling or floor that was altered once the rough wiring was in. Photograph the open framing before it is covered.

Ask the contractor to compare the wiring already installed with the revised layout and to list what stays, what moves and what is added. Ask how the change affects the ESA notification already made.

What should the assessment establish?

Once the layout has changed, wiring already in a Stittsville renovation either still serves its intended space or no longer does. Where it still does, what can stay as installed is explained; where it does not, the contractor identifies each box, switch and receptacle position now in the wrong place and says whether it can be moved or must be replaced. If the new plan adds an outdoor or detached space, that space is covered too. The scope explains how the ESA notification and the building permission reflect the change. Nothing is to be closed in until this revised scope is written.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is rewiring still possible once the drywall is up?

It can be, but it is usually easier before the walls are closed. Ask the contractor what inspection or photographs are needed first, and whether the notification covers the change.

Who updates the drawings when the plan changes mid-project?

Agree this in writing at the start. The person managing the renovation or the contractor can mark the changes, so that the final drawings show what was actually installed and where.

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.

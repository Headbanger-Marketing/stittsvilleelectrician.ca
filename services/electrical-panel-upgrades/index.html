A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Stittsville property

Photograph the panel door from a few steps back to show what stands in front of it, and write down what is stored nearby. Sketch the route from the nearest door, including any step or tight turn.

Ask whether the contractor expects the panel to stay in its present location and what must be cleared or relocated for that. If a pool or hot tub is planned, ask how its circuit would reach the panel.

What should the assessment establish?

Can a panel in an awkward spot stay where it is at all? The report answers that plainly, describing the working space and access the spot offers and what would have to change for it to remain. Does the panel need to move, or would clearing the area in front of it be enough? The contractor's recommendation on that is written down. Where a hot tub or other outdoor equipment is planned for a Stittsville property, how will its circuit reach the panel? That route belongs in the scope, as does anything that could not be seen.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a panel in a blocked spot have to be moved?

Not always. The contractor may find that clearing the area is enough, or that moving it is the better option. Ask for the reasoning in writing so you can compare it with another opinion.

Who should clear the area around the panel before a visit?

Whoever controls the space should do it, whether that is you, a tenant or a condominium corporation. Do not touch the panel itself, and let the contractor say what clearance is needed for the work.

Before work begins

Confirm what is already wired before you depend on it.

Some homes have only a porch light outside; others have a pool, hot tub or outdoor kitchen. There, earlier owners may have left a spare circuit, a weatherproof outlet or a buried cable run, and no one may recall what each was for.

Photograph each outlet and cover you can see from outside, and note what is plugged into it. Ask the seller or a previous contractor for drawings or invoices, then have a contractor confirm each item suits the new load.

How do I find out what an old outdoor circuit was built for?

Look for labels, drawings, invoices and earlier ESA paperwork, and photograph what is visible from the outside. A contractor can then assess the circuit itself, because a label or a seller's memory does not confirm what is actually connected.

Can a hot tub or pool use a circuit that is already there?

Possibly, but that is for the contractor to decide, not the equipment brochure. Outdoor equipment may need different protection from an indoor load, so ask what the existing circuit was installed for and what has changed since.

How should I compare quotes that reuse existing wiring?

Have each contractor list which existing parts they intend to rely on and what they checked to decide that. A quote that is silent on reuse leaves the real scope unclear, so compare those lines first.

Who must agree before a cable crosses my landscaped yard?

It depends on where the route runs and who owns the land: a neighbour, a condominium corporation or the municipality may need to agree. Note any buried services and finished surfaces, then ask how the route will be restored.
